How it works

Two apps, one loop.

Tourism concentrates millions of people on the same ten streets. Journey Walker opens the other ninety percent of the city — through the people who live there.

1

A resident walks

With the recording app, a resident walks two hours through their own neighbourhood — their shortcuts, their stops, what you don't see on the first pass. Zero questions during the walk; five minutes of debrief at the end.

2

The route becomes a walk

The trace, the stops and the resident's own words become a guided walk — reviewed, curated, and anchored to the real ground it was walked on.

3

A visitor follows — freely

The companion app guides at the visitor's pace. Every stop is an invitation, never an order: skip it, linger, wander off. The walk belongs to the walker.

Principles

Built on three sentences.

“The itinerary is the interview.”

We never sit residents down for questionnaires. Where they walk, where they pause, what they say at the end — that is the knowledge, captured in its natural form.

“Every stop is an invitation.”

No turn-by-turn tyranny. The app suggests, reveals, and steps aside. Divergence from the route isn't an error — sometimes it's a resident's shortcut worth learning from.

“We record the path, never the person.”

Recording starts on an explicit tap, once in the street — never at a doorstep. No profile, no tracking, traces deleted after aggregation. Privacy is a design principle, not a settings page.

Business model

Free for walkers. Funded by the street itself.

Sponsors fund routes → routes recruit walkers → walkers create routes.

Each certified route is a corridor of local visibility: the cafés, bookshops and workshops along the way sponsor the walk that brings them exactly the visitors they want — people who move slowly and look around. The flywheel is local by construction, and replicates city by city.
